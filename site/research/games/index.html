Making games got easier. Making one that matters didn’t.

We track what changes when anyone can build, copy or remix a game, and what still makes players stay.

What we’re seeing

  • Players find games through people.

    How 16,152 Steam players most often find new games. Several answers allowed; the sample leans towards dedicated players.

    • Long-form YouTube57%
    • Steam55.8%
    • Word of mouth44.9%
    • Discord17.9%

    Each bar uses the same 0 to 100% scale. The answers overlap and do not add up to 100%.

    Source: GameDiscoverCo Steam Fan Snapshot, September 2026

  • Copying got cheap, so studios stopped showing.

    Digital Sun moved ReVamp’s first playtest from Steam to its Discord. Alliance Arts kept its Next Fest demo to closed sessions. Both cited cloning fears.

    Source: Kotaku, 19 Oct 2026 · Ars Technica, 19 Oct 2026

  • One capability, five responses.

    AI tools now help outsiders rebuild existing games to run in a browser. Five ways the industry answered:

    WhoWhat they did
    OutsidersUnofficial browser ports of big games, Halo among them
    ActivisionReportedly sent DMCA takedowns for AI-built Black Ops 2 browser ports
    Xbox LabsSet up to learn from it and “measure success by whether more people are playing and creating”
    Indie studiosMoved playtests to Discord and closed sessions
    Wonder InteractiveStrata turns Unreal games into browser links for demos and playtests

    Source: The Verge, 13 Oct 2026 · TweakTown, from the developer’s own post · Xbox Wire, 13 Oct 2026 · GamesBeat, 22 Oct 2026

  • Platforms point the same way.

    Valve: community is the first defence against clones, but not enough alone. Keep growing it your way. Visibility follows how many people buy and play.

    Source: Valve at Steam Indie Connect Busan, 27 Oct 2026, as reported by Inven Global

Our working hypothesis

A clone copies a build. It can’t copy the players who shaped it, or the studio they trust.
