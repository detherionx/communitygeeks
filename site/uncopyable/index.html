Small studios before launch

Teams of 1–10 with a game in development and no audience yet.

  • Hoping launch day, Steam or the algorithm will find your players.
  • Few people play your builds, and feedback is scattered.
  • Little time or budget for building relationships.

Training and practical preparation. No publishing, funding, paid ads or guaranteed creator coverage.

Four parts, from first invitation to first creator partner

  1. Find your first fans

    Discover where your potential players already gather and how to bring the first interested people into development.

    Example: Find the forums, Discords and subreddits where your kind of player already talks, and plan a first invitation worth answering.

  2. Get people talking about your game

    Give players reasons to participate, return, share ideas and become advocates for the game.

    Example: Give the people who joined a reason to come back: a new build to try, a question about a design choice, a devlog that answers them.

  3. Build with your players

    Set up simple playtesting and feedback loops that help improve the game, using the communities and tools that make sense.

    Example: Run a closed playtest with a small invited group, collect what confused them, decide what to act on and tell them what changed.

  4. Partner with small creators

    Build genuine relationships with relevant Twitch and YouTube creators through early access, feedback and shared content.

    Example: Choose a few small creators who fit your game and offer early access, a feedback role or a stream you plan together.

Give players a reason to return

A playtest starts the conversation. What you do with the feedback keeps it going.

  1. Invite

    Ask a few relevant players to try your build.

  2. Play

    Let them play. Notice where they get stuck.

  3. Listen

    Ask what worked and what confused them.

  4. Improve

    Choose what to change. Keep creative direction.

  5. Follow up

    Show what changed and invite another playtest.

Repeat with the next build.

Players who care, before launch

Why “Uncopyable”? A mechanic can be copied.

A shared history is harder to copy.

That history is the trust you earn with your players and what you learn together.

Worried about getting cloned?

You don’t have to share everything with everyone. We help you decide what to show, who to involve and when to open up: for example, trusted players first, a small invited playtest next, a wider demo when you’re ready. The people who know your game are also the first to spot a copy.

What studios ask us

Will this get me a publisher?

No one can promise that. You’ll have players who responded and what you learned with them, which can strengthen a pitch.

Won’t showing my game early get it copied?

Cloning existed long before AI, and Valve’s own advice is to build trust with players and keep improving the game. You decide who sees what and when. Many studios start with a small trusted group, then a gated playtest, before going public. The programme helps you weigh what each step gives you and what it costs.

Do I have to use AI?

No. Every exercise works without it. Where an AI tool saves time on research or drafting, we show how. Never for your art.

Do I need a Discord server?

No. Discord is the least common way players discover games (18% in GameDiscoverCo’s 2026 Steam survey). We start where your players already are.

Build something people care about. That’s harder to copy.
